Support

If your question is not here, email us and a person answers, usually the same day.

Somebody asked me to be part of their Seal. What do I do?

Install it, then set it up together the next time you are actually with them. It takes about two minutes and it happens once. Seal only connects two people who are in the same room, so there is nothing to accept remotely and no request waiting for you somewhere.

After that, your job is to keep Seal on your phone and to still be findable in ten years. That is the whole thing. It may be years before anybody needs you, and that is good news.

Can I open somebody’s envelopes?

No, and neither can anyone else, including us. The key that opens them is split into pieces and you hold one. Your piece by itself tells you nothing about the key, not part of it and not a hint of it. That is arithmetic, not a promise we are making.

The exception is if the person who asked you chose a rule that needs only one person. In that case the app tells you so, in those words, on the day you accept and every time you look. If it says you can act alone, you can.

What is Seal for?

The things that cannot go in a will, because a court makes a will public. Passwords. The phrase that opens a crypto wallet. Where the safe deposit key is. The combination. Also the letters, which most people find are the part they actually came for.

What if I am in hospital, not dead?

Then you open the app once and everything stops, cold. Nothing happens for ninety days of silence, then three weeks of daily warnings, then a further quiet period, and one tap ends any of it. You do not need a key or anything else for that tap. Opening the app is enough.

I got a new phone. Is anything lost?

Sign in on the new phone with the same passkey or security key and your identity comes with you. Two things do not.

Your own drafts and media live on the phone that wrote them, so a new phone starts empty. And if you hold a piece of somebody else’s key, that piece was locked to your old phone and cannot move. Tell the person whose Seal you are part of, and ask them to seal again. It takes them one tap and it puts a fresh piece on your new phone. Until they do, you cannot help them.

I lost my phone entirely.

If you added a backup key, sign in with it. Your name and your seal come back, so your family can still check it is really you, but your envelopes and your people do not: they were on the phone you lost. You add your people again, in person, and seal again.

If you did not add a backup key, the identity is gone and nobody, including us, can bring it back. There is no password and no reset, because there is nothing anybody could reset. Add a backup key today if you have not.

Why do I have to meet people in person?

It is the only moment you can be certain the key your phone remembers is really theirs. A name, a number and a voice can all be faked from anywhere. Standing next to somebody cannot.

Does anything I write leave my phone?

Not until you tap Seal, and after that only as ciphertext nobody can read. The part where Seal asks you questions and helps you write is entirely on the phone, including talking instead of typing. There is no account and no server in it. If a phone cannot do that work on its own, Seal does not offer it on that phone rather than sending your words somewhere.

Can I change my rule or my people later?

Yes, at any time, as often as you like. Changing who holds a piece of the key means sealing again, which issues fresh pieces and makes the old ones useless. Your envelopes themselves are not touched.

How do I delete everything?

Open your profile, the round name badge at the top left, then Delete identity. That removes you from the directory permanently and wipes the app. If you have sealed envelopes it asks first whether to keep them for your family or cancel them. It cannot be undone, because there is nothing left to restore. If an old key is blocking you from setting up a new identity, there is a “Retire it” link on the sign in screen for exactly that.

Where is my data, and does it use my iCloud space?

Sealed envelopes and the signed record are stored in Seal’s own CloudKit container on Apple’s servers, as ciphertext. That is not your personal iCloud storage: it does not count against your iCloud space, it does not appear in Settings under Manage Storage, and nothing there can be deleted from Apple’s tools. Only the app, signed by your key, can remove your records. Your own drafts, photos and recordings stay on the phone that wrote them. What is stored, in full.

Does Seal work with iCloud turned off?

No. There is no server of ours, so Apple’s iCloud is the only place a sealed envelope can go. Without an iCloud account signed in on the phone you can write drafts, but you cannot seal or receive. If only iCloud Keychain is off, everything works; you just need a hardware key or your backup key to move to a new phone, because a passkey cannot sync without Keychain.

Which iPhone do I need?

Seal runs on iPhone and iPad from iOS 18. Two parts need iOS 26: the post-quantum half of the encryption, and the help with writing that runs on the phone. Below that, wraps made to your device use the classical half alone, and the writing help is not offered. There is no Android version and no web version.

Do I need a hardware security key?

No. Seal works with a passkey and Face ID or Touch ID. A physical security key is an option for people who want one, and it is the stronger choice against phishing, but nothing in Seal requires it. Note that security keys are the less tested path today.

What does it cost, and where do I get it?

Nothing today. Seal is a free TestFlight beta until Apple approves it for the App Store, and this page links to that version once it is live. After that it is one purchase, once, for life, and the price is shown inside the app before you pay. There is no subscription because there is no server of ours to keep paying for. Holding a key for somebody else, or receiving an envelope, is free: only the person writing envelopes pays.
