Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 16, 2026

Seal holds the things people cannot afford to have read early. So this policy is written to be checked rather than trusted, and it says what leaks as plainly as it says what does not.

What we can never see

Your letters, your photos, your voice messages and your secrets are encrypted on your phone before they leave it. The keys live in your phone's secure hardware and are never transmitted. Seal has no server of its own and no ability to decrypt anything. There is no password, no account recovery and no mechanism by which we, Apple, or anyone else can read an envelope or restore your identity.

That is a statement about what is technically possible, not a promise about our intentions. We could not comply with a demand to hand over an envelope, because we do not hold anything that would satisfy it.

What is stored, and where

Seal uses Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) as a filing cabinet, under our developer account. It holds:

  • Your public identity record: the display name you chose, your public keys, and which kind of key you registered with. This is public on purpose, so the people you meet can check it is you.
  • Encrypted envelope contents, as unreadable ciphertext.
  • A signed, hash linked record of what happened and when: an estate was created, a rule was set, somebody checked in, somebody started a claim, somebody stopped it. The entries are signed and their contents are encrypted.
  • Invitations, encrypted and addressed by a one way hash of the recipient.

What the record leaks, stated plainly

Perfect hiding is not one of the promises, so here is the shape of what an onlooker with access to the stored data could work out:

  • How many people you wrote envelopes to, and roughly how much you wrote.
  • That a particular identity has some part in some estate, because invitations are addressed by hash.
  • When things happened, from the timing of the records.

What they could not work out: the titles, the letters, the secrets, who any envelope is for, or which person holds which role.

Helping you write, which never leaves the phone

Seal can ask you questions and turn your answers into a draft, and it can turn speech into text so you can talk instead of typing. Both run entirely on your phone. There is no account, no server and no upload in either, and nothing you write or say there is sent to us, to Apple, or to anyone else. If a phone cannot do this work on its own, Seal does not offer it on that phone rather than sending the work elsewhere.

The one feature that contacts anybody else

Independent timestamps are off until you turn them on, and they are the only part of Seal that talks to a service outside Apple. With them on, Seal sends a short cryptographic hash of a record entry to a public timestamping authority, currently freetsa.org, which signs the time and sends the signature back. The hash is a one way fingerprint: it does not contain and cannot be turned back into your envelope, your name or anything you wrote.

What it does reveal to that service is that something was written down, and when. That is the whole reason the switch is off until you ask for it, and the setting says so in those words.

Notifications

Seal sends notifications through Apple's push service so the people who hold part of your key hear about a claim. The notification text is written on the phone that shows it, not by us, and it carries nothing about the contents of anything.

What we collect about you

Nothing beyond the above. Seal has no analytics, no trackers, no advertising identifiers, no crash reporting service and no third party code that phones home. We do not collect your phone number, your email address, your contacts or your location, and the app never asks for them. We cannot see who your people are.

Reports

If you report somebody, the report goes from your own mail app to our support address and includes the identities involved. We act on valid reports within 24 hours. Blocking somebody is stored only on your device.

Deleting your identity

You screen, then Delete identity. Your record is permanently marked as deleted in the directory and the app wipes itself on that phone. This cannot be undone and cannot be reversed by us, because deletion is enforced by a write once marker rather than a flag we could flip back.

Encrypted data already delivered to another person's device belongs to that device, as with any letter you have already handed over.

Children

Seal is not directed at children under 13. An identity is created with a passkey or a hardware key belonging to a specific person.
