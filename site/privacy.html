Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 25, 2026

Seal is built so that we can't read your messages — not "we promise not to," but "we are technically unable to." This policy explains exactly what exists, where.

What we can never see

Your messages, photos, and Sealed Cards are end-to-end encrypted on your device before they leave it. The keys live in your phone's secure hardware and are never transmitted. Seal has no server of its own and no ability to decrypt anything you send. There is no password, no account recovery, and no mechanism by which we, Apple, or anyone else can restore access to your identity or read your conversations.

What is stored, and where

Seal uses Apple's iCloud (CloudKit) as a mailbox. It carries only: encrypted message data (unreadable ciphertext), your public identity record — the display name you choose, your public keys, and your account tier — and encrypted media attachments. The display name is the one piece of information you provide that is visible to others; it appears in Seal's public directory so friends can verify you. Choose any name you like.

What we collect about you

Nothing else. Seal has no analytics, no trackers, no advertising identifiers, no crash-reporting service, and no third-party SDKs that phone home. We do not collect your phone number, email, contacts, or location — the app never asks for them.

Reports and blocking

If you report a message, the report is sent from your own mail app to our support address, including the message you flagged and the identities involved. We act on valid reports within 24 hours, up to permanently disabling the offending identity. Blocking someone is stored only on your device.

Disappearing messages

When a chat has disappearing messages on, messages are deleted from your device on schedule and their encrypted copies become permanently unreadable. As with any messaging app, we can't prevent a recipient from photographing or screenshotting a message before it disappears — Seal shows a notice when a screenshot is detected.

Deleting your identity

Profile → Delete identity removes your record from the public directory permanently. Encrypted data already delivered to your friends' devices is theirs, as with any conversation. Deletion cannot be undone — there is nothing left to restore.

Children

Seal is not directed at children under 13, and identities are created with a hardware key or passkey that belongs to a specific person.
