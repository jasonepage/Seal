What Seal does not do

What is not built, what is not promised, and what an attacker gets. The common objections have their own page.

Not done yet, as of September 2026

No independent auditNobody outside this project has reviewed the cryptography or the code. That is the single biggest reason to keep a live seed phrase out of it for now. The source is open so that this can change.
Not on the App StoreSubmitted for review in September 2026 and still waiting. Until it is approved, Seal is a free TestFlight beta. Expect rough edges.
One design review, not an auditOne pass over the key handling, the release machine and the record, by the same assistant that wrote much of the code, which is a weaker check than a stranger. It found two things worth fixing: a key holder could date a claim in the past and skip the warning period, and sealing again after a release could let one recipient read new envelopes early. Both are fixed in the build that was submitted. The write-up is docs/REVIEW.md. That was a review, not an audit.
One personBuilt by one developer. No company, no team and no funding, which means nobody to be acquired by and also nobody to review a mistake before you see it.
Security keys are barely testedA fix for a PIN handling conflict with FIDO2.1 keys is in, and has not been checked on real hardware over NFC or USB-C. The interop table in docs/SECURITY_KEY_TEST_MATRIX.md has no rows filled in. Passkeys are the better tested path today.
Registering a key for somebody else has never met real hardwareThis is the one place where private key material leaves a phone: locked under a secret only a physical security key can compute, and stored in the public database. It is new, it has never run against a real key, and docs/PRE_AUDIT.md says it deserves the hardest look of anything here.
Most tests only run on a phoneThere is no test target, because a passkey needs a real app to live in, so most suites run at launch in debug builds and only the developer watches them pass. One suite, SecurityFixTests, is not registered and so does not run at all. The key split and the countdown compile on their own and anybody can run them. docs/TESTS.md lists all 80 tests by name.
No reproducible buildsYou cannot prove the app Apple ships is built from the source you read. That is true of every app on every phone, and it is on the list rather than solved.
Post-quantum only from iOS 26ML-KEM-768 needs iOS 26. A wrap made to a device below that is X25519 alone, so the protection against recording now and decrypting later is per device, not per estate. The wrap records which of the two it used.
The timestamp authority is a placeholderSeal points at a free public authority. docs/RECORD.md section 13 says why that is not yet a defensible choice, and the verifier leaves the question of whether to trust that authority to the reader.
The field arithmetic is not constant timeThe multiply inside the key split branches on the bits of its operand. Shares are only ever handled on their holder’s own device and there is no remote timing channel into it, so this was accepted rather than rewritten. Test vectors are in place if a reviewer disagrees.
Claim reasons and objection notes are in the clearA key holder types them when starting or objecting to a claim. They are signed, not encrypted, and anybody reading the stored record can read them.
iPhone and iPad onlyNo Android. Your working copy of drafts and media lives on one phone; a second device signed into the same identity can read the record but does not carry that working copy.

Want the audit to happen sooner? An outside review costs more than one person has earned from this so far. Anything sent here goes toward it, and the report goes in the source when it is done. PayPal or Venmo.

What Seal does not promise

That silence means death

Silence is silence. A long stay in hospital looks identical from the outside. That is the entire reason the countdown runs for months rather than days, with weeks of daily warnings that one tap ends.

That the people are who you think

Seal proves that the credential acting later is the same one you stood next to. It cannot bind a credential to a passport. That is what notaries do, and we are not one.

That nothing at all is visible

Somebody reading the stored records can work out how many people you wrote to, roughly how much you wrote, who your key holders are, your rule, and when things happened. They cannot work out titles, letters, secrets, or who any envelope is for. The full list of what is readable is on the technical page.

That a stolen unlocked phone is harmless

Secrets are shown only after a Face ID or Touch ID check, and key material is bound to the device. But a thief with your unlocked phone can keep sending heartbeats, which keeps you looking alive. Seal cannot tell that apart from you being alive.

That a letter can reach a child with no phone

An envelope is wrapped to a specific person’s devices, so that person has to have Seal and to have met you. A grandchild who is four today is a real gap. Every fix we know of weakens the isolation promise, so none has been built.

That a date opens anything

An envelope can carry a date, and after the release the recipient’s phone keeps it closed until that day. There is no server and no clock everyone can trust, so the date is a wish the app honours, not a lock. Somebody determined, with the capsule and a computer, could read it sooner.

That one key holder can never act alone

That depends entirely on the rule the owner set. If they chose a threshold of one, one person can release the estate by themselves after the countdown. The app says so in those words, to that person, on the day they accept and every time they look.

That a release can be undone

Once the Estate Key is published the seal is broken. A check-in after that changes nothing.

The threat model

Every row of docs/SDS.md section 7, with the word custodian written as key holder, and the row about acting early set out in full rather than in a clause.

ThreatDefence
Apple or anyone reads an envelopeContent keys sit inside encrypted tables; a table needs the recipient’s device key plus the Estate Key; the Estate Key needs the owner’s device or M shares.
The directory swaps somebody’s keyPinned when you met; enforced on every single fetch; key holders pin each other from the owner’s signed statement.
The directory forges or hides eventsEvery event is device signed and hash linked, and every device endorsement is re-checked. Hiding is detectable only through capsules held by several people and through timestamp tokens.
The directory un-verifies a deviceAn unsigned removal is ignored. Only a revocation signed by the identity itself counts.
A signature from another site is replayedRelying party hash, user presence bit and type are all enforced.
A re-split endorsement evades revocationThe current framing is length framed. The older one is accepted only at exact lengths.
A key holder submits a bad shareA per share commitment in the signed record names them.
Key holders act earlyThere is no cryptographic time lock. Nothing can enforce one without a trusted third party, and Seal has none on purpose. The countdown is a signed public record that honest apps obey. To cheat it, M key holders would have to pull their pieces out with a modified app, and the recipient would have to run one too, because the Estate Key alone opens nothing. So the people you picked, plus the reader, could act together to open early. That is the trust you chose when you chose them, and the record shows it.
The owner is declared dead while aliveSilence, then weeks of warnings, then a quiet period, with a one tap cancel that needs no hardware key, plus objections.
A phone’s clock liesTimestamp tokens on heartbeats, claims, taps and releases; the record prefers the authority’s time, and each phone also refuses to treat a claim as older than the day it first saw it.
Harvest now, decrypt laterML-KEM-768 in every estate wrap, on iOS 26 and above.
The company disappearsThe capsule and its verifier. See for yourself.

One key, one identity, is deterrence only

Registration excludes every credential already in the directory, so an authenticator refuses to mint a second identity. A FIDO2 reset or a modified client gets around that. It is a speed bump, described as a speed bump, and the design does not depend on it.
