Some things can't go in a will.

A will filed for probate becomes a public court record. Anyone can read it. So the password to your bank, the phrase that opens your crypto wallet, where the safe deposit key is, the combination, the thing you never told anybody: none of it can go in one. Seal is where those things go instead.

Join the beta, free How it works

iPhone only for now. Installs through Apple TestFlight.

Nobody can open one early. Not Apple, not us.

This is not a policy we could change our minds about. An envelope is encrypted on your phone before it leaves it, and the key that opens it is split into pieces so that no one person holds it. We do not have a copy. Neither does Apple.

What you do, once

1 · Write the envelopes

One for each person. A letter, a few photos, a voice message, and the secrets: passwords, where the documents are, the combination, the seed phrase. You do not need anyone else to start. Type a name and write tonight.

2 · Choose who can open them

A few people you trust, added face to face. They hold pieces of one key. One piece on its own is no clue at all, not even a hint, so no single person can act alone unless you decide they can.

3 · Set the rule, then tap Seal

How long the silence has to be, how many warnings you get, how many of them it takes. After that, opening the app now and then is the whole ongoing job.

What happens after

  • 1You go quiet. Opening the app is your check-in, and nobody else sees a thing. Nothing starts until you have been silent past the limit you chose, ninety days by default.
  • 2Somebody starts a claim. One of the people you chose says they believe you are gone. Everyone else is told the same day.
  • 3You are warned, every day, for weeks. Three weeks of daily warnings by default, then a further quiet period. One tap from you stops all of it, cold, at any point. You do not need a key for that.
  • 4Only then do the envelopes open. The people you chose act together, and each envelope opens on the phone of the person it was written for. Nobody reads anybody else's.

A long stay in hospital looks exactly like death from the outside. The warnings exist for precisely that, and they are loud on purpose.

Writing it is the hard part, so Seal helps

The way this goes wrong is not the mathematics. It is an empty vault that opens perfectly. Every ceremony worked, every warning ran, the envelopes opened, and there was nothing in them.

So Seal asks you questions, one at a time, and turns your answers into a draft you then edit. You can talk instead of typing, which most people find easier for the part that matters. Nothing you write or say in that part leaves your phone. There is no account and no server in it. Your voice becomes words on the phone itself, or not at all.

What Seal does not promise

That silence means death. Silence is silence. That is the entire reason the warnings run for weeks rather than hours.

That we can vouch for who anybody is. Seal proves that the person acting later is the same person you stood next to. Binding a face to a passport is what notaries are for.

That everything is hidden. The record shows how many people you wrote to and roughly how much you wrote. It does not show the titles, the letters, the secrets, or who is who.

That a letter can reach a child with no phone. An envelope is written to a specific person's device, so that person has to have Seal and to have met you. A grandchild who is four years old today is a real gap, and we would rather say so than pretend.

Built to outlive us

There is no Seal server. The record of who did what, and when, is signed and hash linked, and anyone holding a piece of your key can export an archive of the whole thing. A short script with no Seal in it checks every signature in that archive. If this company disappears tomorrow, the envelopes still open.

On iPhones running iOS 26 the wrapping is hybrid, classical elliptic curve plus ML-KEM-768, so a recording made today is not readable by a quantum computer later. On older phones it is the classical half only, and the app says which you have.
