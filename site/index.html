Sealed envelopes for the people you leave behind.

Seal is an iPhone app for writing one sealed envelope per person: a letter, and the passwords and instructions that person needs. Everything is encrypted on your phone, and an envelope opens only after you have gone quiet for months and weeks of daily warnings have passed. There is no Seal server, the source is public, and nobody outside this project has audited it.

iPhone and iPad. Free to download; one purchase, once, when you seal your first envelope. Key holders and recipients never pay. Open source under MPL 2.0. The limits are here.

After you die, your will goes through a court, and what is in it becomes a public record that anyone can look up. So passwords, seed phrases, safe combinations and private letters cannot go in one. Those are the things Seal holds instead.

What you do, once

  • 1Write the envelopes. One for each person. A letter, a few photos, a voice message, and the secrets. You do not need anybody else to start: type a name and write tonight.
  • 2Choose who can open them. A few people you trust, added face to face. They hold pieces of one key, and one piece on its own is no clue at all, not even a hint.
  • 3Set the rule, then seal. How long the silence, how many days of warnings, how long after that, and how many of them it takes. Ninety days, twenty one, fourteen, any two of three, by default.
  • 4Open the app now and then. That is the entire ongoing job. Opening it is the check-in that keeps everything closed.

What happens after

  • 1You go quiet. Nothing begins until you have been silent past the limit you chose. Nobody else sees your check-ins.
  • 2Somebody starts a claim. One of the people you chose says they believe you are gone. Everybody else is told the same day.
  • 3You are warned, every day, for weeks. Then a further quiet period. One tap from you ends all of it at any point, and that tap needs no key.
  • 4Only then do the envelopes open. The people you chose act together, and each envelope opens on the phone of the person it was written for. Nobody reads anybody else’s, including the people holding the keys.

Writing it is the hard part

The way this goes wrong is not the mathematics. It is an empty vault that opens perfectly. So Seal asks questions, one at a time, and turns your answers into a draft you then edit. You can talk instead of typing, and none of that work leaves the phone.

Somebody asked you to hold a key? Nothing is waiting for you to accept, because this is done in person. Install Seal now and do the rest when you next see them. What holding a key involves.

The objections

Just use a password manager. This has been done before. It is not audited. What if you quit? All fair. Each one gets an answer.

The short technical answer

X25519 plus ML-KEM-768 for wrapping, HKDF-SHA256, AES-256-GCM for content, P-256 ECDSA through WebAuthn for identity, Shamir over GF(256) for the key split, RFC 3161 for timestamps. Apple CryptoKit throughout, no third party dependencies. The long answer is here.
