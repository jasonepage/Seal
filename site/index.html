How Seal works

1 · Set up in person

You and the other person stand together and tap once. That moment is the whole security model, and it is the one thing a stranger on the internet can never copy. No usernames, no phone numbers, nothing that can be stolen remotely.

2 · Send it privately

Everything is encrypted on your phone before it leaves, and signed by the phone that sent it. You can set messages to disappear on a timer, and Seal tells you when somebody takes a screenshot.

3 · Seal what can't be wrong

Wallet addresses, account numbers and payment instructions travel as Sealed Cards. Locked exactly as written, copied character for character, and impossible to mistake for ordinary chat.

Why not just send it normally?

Phone numbers get spoofed. SIM cards get swapped. A voice can be cloned from ten seconds of audio. And a photo sent in an ordinary chat quietly ends up in a cloud backup neither of you controls. Every channel you already use trusts a number or a voice, which are the two things a stranger fakes best. Seal trusts neither. Your identity lives in your phone's secure chip and it was established face to face, so there is nothing to phish and nobody to impersonate.

What Seal does not do

Seal proves a message came from that person's phone. It does not know whether what they are asking for is wise, and it cannot tell you their phone is still in their hands. If a request feels off, call them. That is still a conversation between two people, and we just make sure you are having it with the real one.

Disappearing messages are removed from both phones on schedule. Seal tells you when somebody screenshots a chat, but it cannot stop a second camera pointed at a screen. Nothing can. Send accordingly.

Built different, on purpose

There is no server holding your messages, so there is nothing to breach. There are no passwords and no account resets, so there is nothing to phish. We cannot read anything you send, which also means we could not sell it if we wanted to. iPhone for now, Android on the roadmap.
