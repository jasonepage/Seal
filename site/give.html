01 / Inside the gift

Three things. One clear purpose.

1

Security keys

A small physical key for each person they trust. They set each one up on their iPhone, and it belongs to that person from then on.

2

The Seal app

Free on the App Store. They write a letter, add photos and their voice, and save the passwords. Sealing is a one-time purchase in the app.

3

A printed card

Plain steps. No manual. One more page goes in a drawer with each key, saying what it is for.

02 / The evening

They write it. You can hold a key.

Sit with them while they set it up. They choose who gets an envelope and what goes in it. Seal can ask them questions, one at a time, if the blank page is the hard part, and they can speak their answers instead of typing.

You will not see what they wrote. Holding a key does not let you read anything. Each envelope opens only for the person it was written to.

03 / The point

Nothing opens while they are here.

  1. They go quiet for a long time. 90 days without opening Seal, unless they choose a different rule.
  2. They get weeks of warnings. If they open the app once, everything stops. They do not need a key for that.
  3. Enough key holders agree. Only then do the envelopes open, each on the phone of the person it was written for.

04 / What you are really giving

Not their passwords. Their words.

"I want you to have what you need when I am gone."

A family can end up with locked accounts and no letter. Seal is for the letter, and for the passwords, the bank, and where the will is. It works best when they chose to do it, so ask before you buy.
