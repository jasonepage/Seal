How it works

Two touches. Then it opens.

  1. 1

    Press the button

    Press Use my security key up above.

  2. 2

    Plug in, then touch

    Plug the key in. When it blinks, touch the gold circle.

  3. 3

    Touch once more

    The second touch unlocks your letters. That is it.

Questions

Plain facts.

Where do I touch the key?

Plug the key into your phone or computer. When the key blinks, touch the gold circle on the key.

It is asking for a PIN

Type the PIN the writer gave you with the key. If you were never given one, ask them, or ask whoever helps them with Seal.

Go slowly. After a few wrong tries in a row, most keys ask you to unplug them and start again, and after too many wrong tries in total a key locks for good.

Which phones and computers work?

Almost any phone or computer from the last few years: Android phones, iPhones, Windows, Mac and Chromebooks. Use Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari, kept up to date.

Someone asked me to hold a key for them

Thank you. It means they trust you. Holding a key is mostly waiting: keep the key safe, and once in a while you may be asked to confirm you still have it, here or in the Seal app.

If the writer ever goes quiet for a long time, the key holders together decide whether their letters should open. Seal warns the writer many times first, and they can stop it with one tap.

I would like to write letters of my own

We would love that. Writing and sealing letters happens in the Seal app, which needs an iPhone or iPad that can run iOS 18 or iPadOS 18. There is no Android app yet.

You can still open letters and hold a key for someone from any phone, right here.

Seal on the App Store

I want to give someone a key

The writer does this in the Seal app on their iPhone. They plug in a spare security key, set it up for that person, and hand it over in person, like a house key. Kits are here.

Nothing worked, or I am stuck

Email support@sealmessenger.com. A real person answers, usually the same day. Say what you see on the screen, or send a photo of it. You will never be asked for your PIN or your key.

Is this page safe?

It runs on your device, reads locked data from Apple's servers, and opens it with your key. Nothing we run can read, open or change your letters, and this page saves nothing.

If you hold a key, what you sign here goes to Apple through a small relay of ours. It cannot forge or change what you signed. It can fail to deliver it, or delete it later, so the page checks Apple's copy before it says anything was saved.

A web page is weaker than the app in one way, and we would rather say so. It is fetched fresh on every visit and skips Apple's review, so whoever controlled this website could change it, even for one visitor. It loads no code from anywhere else, its source is public, and the hashes of its files are published to compare. If that is not enough, open your letters in the iPhone app, which never depends on this site.
